A 7-joint arm with the published kinematics of the Franka Emika Panda reaches targets in 3D. Each move is proposed by a planner, checked by a forward-kinematics gate before execution, and remembered if it passed; similar situations replay the remembered move instead of calling the planner. Scenes 5–7 add what the study added next: a look-alike object, a touch check, a camera that can disagree with memory, and a check placed before planning. Pick a scene, or set the switches yourself. What this is and is not. Kinematics follow Franka's published Denavit-Hartenberg parameters and Panda joint limits; links are drawn as cylinders, not the real meshes, and there is no dynamics or collision geometry. The planner is a damped-least-squares step toward the believed target with injected mistakes (30, 50 or 70% wrong) — a stand-in, not an LLM; the check before planning is modelled as lowering that mistake rate to a quarter, using the same believed state. The camera, the look-alike and the touch check are simple models (camera noise 3 mm, gripper-width noise 2 mm). Numbers on this page are illustrative; the measured results are on the study page. With seven joints a fingertip position cannot be turned back into one joint reading, so here the sensor is used directly: the error to the target and the gate's prediction start from the measured fingertip.
