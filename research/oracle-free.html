No oracle in the loop: memory and verification on a Panda

Preliminary study · 2026.10 · self-initiated · MuJoCo Franka Panda · local LLMs (gemma3 4B, qwen2.5-coder 32B) · every run pre-registered, judged on held-out scene seeds

시뮬레이터의 정답값을 결정 경로에서 모두 빼고, 이미지·거리 추적·IMU·촉각만으로 집어 옮기기를 하면서 검증된 기억이 계산을 얼마나 줄이는지, 관절 읽기가 틀릴 때 무엇이 막는지를 본 예비 연구입니다.

My earlier study showed that verified memory cuts LLM calls, and that checks before and after planning are both fooled when the robot's own joint reading is wrong. It still read the box position and the fingertip from the simulator. Here I removed every such shortcut from the decision loop and asked the same two questions again: can memory stand in for a large model, and what keeps the robot right when its own state is wrong?

What "no oracle" means here 결정 경로에 정답값이 없다

What the robot needsHow it gets it
Where the box is, which one, its orientationA camera image (ray-cast, 128 × 128, depth and colour noise, dropped pixels). Boxes are segmented, the top face is fitted, and the target is the one whose short side measures about 32 mm (the look-alike is 40 × 40).
Where the hand isJoint readings through forward kinematics, checked against a range tracker (four anchors, 2 cm noise, occasional non-line-of-sight bias).
Which way the hand pointsAn IMU: gravity in the hand frame, 1° noise.
Whether it holds the right boxFinger opening after closing (touch).

The simulator's ground truth is used only to score the outcome (placed and left standing) and to report errors. Boxes lie in any orientation, so the robot also has to measure how a box is turned and rotate its wrist: without that, 54/108 were placed; with it, 108/108.

1 · Memory stands in for the large model 기억이 큰 모델을 대신한다

The planner is asked one step at a time. Every proposed step is checked by forward kinematics before it runs. Steps that passed and made progress are kept as memory: the situation (fingertip-to-goal vector) and the fingertip move, re-solved by inverse kinematics for the current pose when replayed, and only when the remembered situation is close relative to the distance left. A small model plans; the large one is called only after the small one is rejected twice.

2 · When the robot's own state is wrong 로봇 자신의 상태가 틀릴 때

A shoulder reading off by 0.15 rad fools the gate, the memory and the planner at once, because all three compute the hand from that reading. How I got from collapse to recovery:

  1. Removing the oracle broke the earlier fix. With an ideal fingertip sensor an independent check had solved this; with a realistic range tracker the same robot placed 1/18.
  2. Trust the tracker only when it disagrees. Comparing a 5-read median with the joint-based fingertip, and switching only on a large gap, helped with a good tracker (14 → 17/18) but not with a rough one (1 → 6/18): detecting the error was not enough.
  3. Estimate the error, not just detect it. Calibrating joint offsets from position alone left the hand tilted 3.6–6.5° and boxes fell over; from the IMU alone the fingertip was 3–5 cm off; both together: 0.4–1.3 cm and 0.3–0.5°. Four reviewing models had called the IMU unnecessary.
  4. Calibrate online. Fault with no fix: 0/108 (stand-in planner). Tracker + IMU calibration while moving: 97/108 with the stand-in planner, 100/108 with real LLMs on unseen seeds.
  5. Observe → verify → act. Every remaining failure was the first episode of a run: the robot grasped before calibration had enough observations. A check before the grasp (enough observations, hand position agrees within 5 cm, otherwise look again from nearby points) gave 108/108; first-episode failures 6 → 0; large-model calls 36 → 13.

What a fault does to memory 고장은 기억의 무엇을 더럽히나

I expected memories recorded under the fault to be geometrically wrong and planned to correct them. They were not: stored as task-space moves, they were consistent within the robot's own belief, and replayed steps worked as well as clean ones. What the fault corrupted was the outcome label — every episode failed, so every memory was marked bad and none was replayed. Marking only the fault period's outcomes as invalid, which the tracker–reading gap detects on its own, cut planner calls 89 % compared with resetting memory, on the easier task. On the harder task this helped much less (−23 %), because the faulty robot never got past grasping: all of its memories were approach steps. A fault spoils labels and also leaves gaps in experience, and fixing labels cannot fill them.

What did not work 안 된 것

  • Correcting faulty memories back into true coordinates made them worse: the faulty robot had never been near the goals, so none of the corrected situations were (0 % within 6 cm of a goal).
  • Refusing to calibrate directions the sensors cannot observe (the wrist roll when the hand points down) cleaned up the estimate but placed fewer boxes than the usual calibration on the same seeds (100 vs 104/108) and shifted another joint. Dropped.
  • Rotating boxes by up to ±45° did not make the task harder: the grip turned them square. Only any orientation did.
Simulation only. Sensors are noise models, the camera has no shadows or reflections, one kind of fault (a fixed shoulder offset), and two to six held-out seeds per comparison. Memory helps here because evaluation scenes resemble the ones memory was built on; I have not tested a different kind of task.

What I want to study next 다음 연구

쉬운 과제에서 쌓은 기억과 검증으로 더 어려운 과제를 넘을 수 있는지, 그리고 고장이 남긴 경험의 빈칸을 어떻게 채울지를 보고 싶습니다.

Two questions came out of this. Can memory and checks learned on an easy task carry a robot through a harder one, and what fills the gaps a fault leaves in experience? The rule I would keep regardless: observe, verify, then act, at every switch from one phase to the next, not only at every step.

Setup 설정

MuJoCo 3.15, Franka Panda from MuJoCo Menagerie. Local models through Ollama at temperature 0 on an Apple M5 Pro laptop. Each comparison had its pass criteria written before running; selection was done on tuning seeds and judged once on seeds not used before. Code, logs and the pre-registrations are kept with the project.
