eLoran Coherent Accumulation under Random Sample Loss (NAVIS Lab)

2025.12 – present

Designed two compensation algorithms to rescue time-of-arrival estimation when a receiver randomly drops samples — then showed with a 1,000-run Monte-Carlo that neither one beats doing nothing.

  • Separated the two loss mechanisms and modelled them as statistically independent: zero-substitution from protective blanking, which costs energy, and sample concatenation from DMA overrun, which silently shortens the time axis and injects timing jitter — the fatal one, since a single dropped sample rotates the phase of everything after it.
  • Time domain (G18): six-stage pipeline — BPF and validity masking, cross-correlation lag estimation, a PSR-based quality gate that locks out unrecoverable frames, circshift jitter cancellation, IIR recursive accumulation, and reliability-weighted (p³) regeneration against a reference template.
  • Frequency domain (G21): started on the S-Transform and abandoned it — when the time index itself is corrupted, a transform that folds that index into its window amplifies the phase error instead of preserving it. Rebuilt on FFT: Wiener + Gaussian amplitude masks, a phase-coherence mask, and an accumulation weight driven by a per-frame quality factor.
  • Evaluated at 50% random sample loss over 1,000 Monte-Carlo runs — error CDFs, running-mean bias convergence, and jitter histograms per station, rather than a single favourable trial.

Result: G21 bought +6.78 dB of SNR and paid for it with a permanent +1.497 µs TOA bias and cycle slips at the secondary stations — error variance worsened by 146–219%. G18 overfitted, substituting one pulse out of nine, and landed TOA 1.35 ms from truth; it was excluded from the statistical run. Plain accumulation won. Higher SNR is not the objective — a correct arrival time is, and reconstructing samples that were never received corrupts the 100 kHz carrier phase permanently.

Imagination Learning — Finding & Verifying Where LLM/VLM Robot Control Breaks (KAERI)

2026 · ~6 mo intern

Pinpointing the failure modes of zero/few-shot LLM-VLM control — and building ways to check for them.

  • Designed controlled ablations (DOF 1→2→5; hints No-hint / CoT / joint-info / image feedback; VLM read-vs-generate) to isolate where spatial reasoning fails.
  • Surfaced concrete failure signals: joint-info hints hurt (165.7mm → 276.9mm), a Verifier ceiling at 5-DOF, and same-family self-verification bias.
  • Ran the full research loop solo — experiment design, data collection, visualization, and the report submitted to the lab.

What it establishes: a model checking itself is not enough. Oral presentation + proceedings, Korea Society of Industrial Information Systems 2026. The project closed in 2026; that negative result is what moved me toward independent, physically-grounded checks.

FedV9 — Noise-Robust Object Detection via Heterogeneous Relational KD

2025.11 – present · team lead

Keeping a lightweight detector accurate under satellite sensor noise by distilling a foundation model's relational structure.

  • Distill DINOv2 (ViT) → YOLOv8s-seg (CNN) across a heterogeneous gap via distance-wise relational KD + 50:50 mixed-noise training.
  • Built the full ML pipeline solo: RKD loss, custom Ultralytics trainer (SPPF hook + adapter + mixed noise), and 3 framework-level bug fixes.
  • Teacher & adapter detached at inference → zero added cost.

Result (σ=0.3, iSAID): noisy mAP50 0.009 → 0.196, clean 0.278 → 0.426; deployed model 11.8M / 22MB.
