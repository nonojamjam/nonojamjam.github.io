I study when a robot can trust what its AI perceives and plans — and how much it should re-check. At KAERI I built a forward-kinematics gate for VLM robot commands and an LLM wiki the agent writes and reads as its own memory. I am now testing how verified experience can cut computation, and what happens when the robot's own state estimate is wrong.

Preliminary study 예비 연구

Memory and verification on a Panda, without an oracle

2026.10 · self-initiated · MuJoCo Panda · local LLMs (4B and 32B) · pre-registered, held-out scenes · two steps

Step 2 (current). The robot sees only through a camera image, a range tracker, an IMU and touch. With a 32B model's verified memory, a 4B planner matched the 32B planner (36/36) with zero large-model calls. With a shoulder reading 0.15 rad off, independent sensors plus "observe, verify, then act" took the robot from 0 to 108/108. On a harder level (a wall, a box to stack on, both), reusing that memory cut large-model calls by 78–96 % with every box placed. A third level (clutter, occlusion, two boxes in a row, a fault mid-run) is tuned and runs in the live demo; its held-out evaluation is next.

Step 1. With box and fingertip positions read from the simulator, verified memory removed most LLM calls at the same success, and a touch check stopped wrong memories. When the robot's own state was wrong, checks before and after planning were both fooled, which is what step 2 set out to fix.
